        What the numbers mean
        
  
        Sound
        
  
        
    
        dB, decibels
        
    
        How loud something is. 10 dB more sounds about twice as loud, and you lose 6 dB each time you double your distance from the sound.
        
    
        dB(A)
        
    
        Loudness the way a sound meter reads it.
      
        
        
        30a quiet bedroom at night
        
        
        40a quiet room, a library
        
        
        50a quiet office, light rain
        
        
        60talking, 3 ft away
        
        
        70a vacuum cleaner
        
        
        80a busy street, at the curb
        
      
        
    
        
    
        dBFS, the raw level
        
    
        The level inside a microphone's own recording, from about −90 (silence) to 0 (the loudest it can record). It only compares sounds on the same device.
        
    
        Setting the meter
        
    
        Turns a phone's raw level into dB(A). Once per phone, at home: the detector on USB to a laptop running the logger, the phone beside the detector's mic, the calibration playing, then tap Set on the laptop.
        
  
        
  
        Results
        
  
        
    
        Aimed and measured
        
    
        The level the logger tried to play at the mic, and what the mic phone heard. Below the background, measured reads the background.
        
    
        Radio signal, dBm
        
    
        Closer to zero is stronger: −40 is very strong, −100 is fine, about −120 is the limit.
        
    
        Radio clarity, dB
        
    
        How far the message stood above radio noise. It still works down to about −15.
